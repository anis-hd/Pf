Fruit-Fly Connectome Digital Twin — How It Works
==================================================

1. WHAT THE PROJECT IS
----------------------
A real-time spiking digital twin of the adult male fruit-fly central nervous
system. The anatomical wiring comes from the Male CNS v1.0 connectome
(~211k annotated neurons, ~152M synapses), and every neuron is simulated as
a spiking unit. There is no trained controller. Behavior emerges from real
synaptic weights plus live sensory drive.

The brain is a custom spiking network built directly on the connectome.
The body and environment are a physically simulated fly that provides
rich touch, smell, taste, visual, wind, temperature, humidity and
proprioception senses, and legs that walk.

2. HOW THE BRAIN WORKS
----------------------
2.1 Connectome graph:
The brain network is the real fly wiring diagram. Each node is a neuron,
each edge is a synaptic connection with a weight equal to synapse count.
Weak, self, and unannotated connections are filtered out, leaving millions
of strong edges. The result is a large sparse directed graph of tens of
thousands of connected neurons.

Each neuron gets a synaptic sign from its predicted neurotransmitter:
acetylcholine-like neurons excite (+1), GABA / glutamate / histamine-like
neurons inhibit (-1), modulatory transmitters like serotonin, dopamine and
octopamine are treated as weakly excitatory. Missing predictions default
to excitatory. This turns the anatomical map into a signed, functional
network with a realistic excitatory / inhibitory balance.

Incoming synapses to each neuron are normalized so no neuron is
overwhelmed just because it has many inputs: each neuron's total input
strength is scaled to a common gain. This keeps network activity stable
and lets stimulus responses stand out.

2.2 Neuron dynamics:
Each neuron is a leaky integrate-and-fire unit updated every 1 ms of
simulated time, with a membrane time constant around 10 ms, a firing
threshold, a reset to zero after spiking, background noise, and a short
2 ms refractory period where it cannot fire again.

Each step:
  input current = sum over all spiking presynaptic neurons of their signed,
  normalized weight, plus external sensory / stimulus current, plus noise.
  voltage decays slightly, then adds that current.
  if voltage crosses threshold and the neuron is not refractory, it spikes,
  resets, and enters refractoriness.

Spikes are binary events. The whole-brain state is just voltages and a
spike vector, computed as one large sparse matrix multiply per step,
on GPU if available.

2.3 Functional zones:
Sensory and motor neurons are identified from biological annotations
(cell type, nerve, brain region, receptor labels). Examples: olfactory,
visual, gustatory, mechanosensory, chordotonal / Johnston's organ,
thermosensory, hygrosensory and ascending neurons as sensory; motor
neurons as motor, with graph in/out-degree as fallback.

From these, anatomical pools are built:
  Sensory channels (12): smell, left, right, touch, taste, wind, light,
  warm, cool, left proprioception, right proprioception, humidity.
  For example smell comes from antennal-nerve sensory neurons, touch from
  leg nerves, taste from mouthpart nerve, wind from Johnston's /
  chordotonal organs, vision from visual projection and optic sensory
  classes, warmth from VP2 and cold from VP3 thermosensory types,
  proprioception from chordotonal / campaniform / bristle / hair-plate
  neurons split by left/right side, humidity from hygrosensory neurons.
  Each body sense drives only its matching biological subset.

  Motor pools: leg motor neurons split by segment T1/T2/T3 and left/right,
  plus aggregated left-leg and right-leg pools; descending neurons from
  brain to nerve cord as a whole and split left/right; abdominal motor
  neurons; head motor neurons; and whole-motor left/right halves by cell
  body side. Mean spike rate in each pool is what steers the body.

A monitored subset of about 1200 neurons (all sensory + all motor + a
sample of interneurons) is tracked for live display, grouped as
sensory | interneurons | motor.

3. HOW THE BODY WORKS — SENSORS
-------------------------------
The body is a physics-based fly with six legs walking on flat or blocky
terrain in an arena containing a food berry, a warm spot, a cool spot and
a humid spot. Each simulation step it reports senses as 0..1 levels:

  smell: falls with distance to food, 1 when on top of it.
  left / right smell: smell split by whether food is to the left or right
  of the current heading, giving stereo olfaction for turning toward food.
  touch: ground contact force on the feet, reported as total plus left and
  right maxima.
  taste: 1 while within eating distance of the berry, else 0. Eating fills
  a satiety value, then the berry respawns elsewhere.
  speed / wind: thorax speed from frame-to-frame displacement, used both as
  walking speed and wind / airflow sense.
  light: baseline plus speed-dependent optic-flow proxy.
  warm / cool: virtual temperature field — ambient 25 C with a warm Gaussian
  bump (~33 C) and a cool dip (~17 C). Converted to separate warm and cool
  channels.
  humid: virtual humidity field with a moist peak decaying to ambient dry.
  proprioception left/right: from leg joint angles and velocities versus a
  neutral stance pose, averaged separately for left and right legs. Bends
  and moves more -> higher signal.

These levels are converted back into brain current: each channel Poisson-
drives only its anatomical sensory subset, stronger level = higher spike
probability per millisecond. That closes the loop from world to brain.

4. HOW THE BODY WORKS — ACTUATORS
---------------------------------
The fly has 42 active leg joint drives plus switchable foot adhesion.
Walking comes from a tripod central pattern generator: six oscillators
(left-front, left-mid, left-hind, right-front, right-mid, right-hind) in
anti-phase tripod pattern, mapped through preprogrammed leg kinematics
to joint angles, with adhesion on/off timed to stance phase.

The brain does not set joints directly. It sets four high-level drives:
left amplitude, right amplitude, turn, and speed. Mapping:

  motor spike fractions are read from the brain (left vs right motor,
  leg pools, descending pools). Very small fractions (~0..0.03) are
  expanded by a recruitment curve to 0..1 amplitudes, so tiny changes in
  firing recruit visibly more movement.
  left amplitude follows left descending / left leg / left motor activity,
  right amplitude mirrors it. Speed follows overall descending / motor
  activity. Turn follows the left-right difference, with a deadband so
  small asymmetries go straight.
  All drives are smoothed over time so the gait does not jitter.

Speed selects a gait gear: slow amble ~5 Hz, walk ~8 Hz, fast stride
~12 Hz, with hysteresis so it does not flicker. Each brain millisecond
advances several physics substeps of oscillator + joints + adhesion +
rigid-body simulation. The body auto-settles to stance at start, stays
quiet briefly, and respawns if tipped over or in an invalid state.

5. HOW THE SIMULATION WORKS
---------------------------
Time runs in 1 ms brain steps, typically 1200 steps per run.

Each step, in order:
  1. Build external current: scheduled stimulus windows (e.g. Poisson
     bursts into a random fraction of sensory neurons), plus any manual
     sensory-burst or whole-brain-burst button press, plus body-sense
     drive from the 12 channels described above.
  2. Step all LIF neurons once: sparse network current + external current
     + noise -> spikes.
  3. Measure mean spike rates: whole population, whole motor pool, and
     each leg / descending / abdominal / head pool.
  4. Convert motor rates to left/right/speed/turn drives, step the
     pattern generator and physics, read back new senses for the next step.
  5. Every N steps, sample the monitored neurons and append to scrolling
     histories.

If mean activity exceeds ~50% the run is flagged as runaway seizure-like
activity. At the end of a run, baseline motor rate before stimulation is
compared to post-stimulation rate to quantify whether the stimulus drove
movement (e.g. several-fold increase).

An offline mode runs the same brain without the body on fixed stimulus
windows and saves a raster image and spike archive. The live mode adds
the body closed loop, manual stimulation, speed control, and live tuning
of threshold, membrane time constant, noise, gain and stimulus strength.

6. HOW THE VISUALIZATION WORKS
------------------------------
Every flash is a real simulated spike from the monitored set.

  3D brain: one point per neuron at its real cell-body location, colored
  by flash on spike and tinted by cell class. Background neurons give
  anatomical context. Rotatable, with a top-down projection view.

  Spike raster: rows are monitored neurons in sensory | interneuron |
  motor order (with divider lines), columns are time. A dot is a spike.
  Stimulus windows are highlighted so sensory-driven bands and the
  following motor response are visible.

  Rate plots: scrolling whole-population rate and motor-pool rate, plus
  per-pool bars for left leg, right leg, descending command, and leg
  segments T1/T2/T3.

  Body view: live follow-camera video of the physics fly, plus telemetry
  — distance to food, amount eaten, touch, speed, temperature, humidity,
  left/right proprioception, left/right drive amplitudes, and arena
  position. Controls allow run, pause, reset, stimulate sensory,
  stimulate whole brain, drop food, and reset the body.
